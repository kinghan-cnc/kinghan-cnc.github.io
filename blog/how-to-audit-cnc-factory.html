How to Audit a CNC Factory Before Placing Orders

February 27, 2026 · 11 min read
← Back to Blog

Placing a significant order with a CNC machine shop you've never worked with is inherently risky. Parts might arrive out of spec, deliveries may be late, or the factory may lack the capacity to handle your volume. A pre-production factory audit eliminates most of these risks by verifying the supplier's capabilities, quality systems, and operational readiness before you commit.

This guide walks you through how to conduct an effective CNC factory audit — whether on-site or remote — covering every critical area from equipment to quality management.

Why Factory Audits Matter for CNC Parts Buyers

A factory audit is not about distrust — it's about due diligence. Even experienced buyers have been burned by suppliers who look great on paper but can't deliver in practice. An audit helps you:

  • Verify actual capabilities — confirm the equipment list matches what's really on the shop floor
  • Assess quality systems — ensure there are real processes behind the ISO certificate
  • Evaluate capacity — determine if the shop can handle your volume without overcommitting
  • Identify risks early — spot red flags before they become expensive problems
  • Build relationships — face-to-face visits create trust and open communication channels

Pre-Audit Preparation

Before visiting or conducting a remote audit, gather and review:

  • Company profile: Years in business, ownership structure, number of employees, key customers
  • Certifications: ISO 9001, IATF 16949, AS9100 — request copies and verify with the certification body
  • Equipment list: Request a detailed machine list including brand, model, year, and quantity
  • Sample parts: Request sample parts or photos of similar work they've produced
  • References: Ask for 2–3 customer references you can contact

This pre-screening helps you decide whether an audit is worth the investment and lets you focus your on-site time on verification rather than basic information gathering.

The 7 Key Areas to Audit

1. Equipment and Machinery

The shop floor tells the real story. During your audit, verify:

  • Machine brands and models: Are they using quality brands (Citizen, Star, Tsugami, Mazak) or low-end equipment?
  • Machine age and condition: Well-maintained older machines can be excellent, but neglected equipment is a red flag
  • Capacity utilization: Are machines running or sitting idle? Both extremes can be problematic
  • Maintenance records: Do they have a preventive maintenance schedule?
  • Technology match: Do they have the right type of equipment for your parts? For precision turned parts under 32mm, look for Swiss-type CNC lathes

2. Quality Management System

Having an ISO certificate is a start, but you need to verify the quality system is actually implemented:

  • Incoming material inspection: How do they verify raw material? Do they check mill certs and do material verification?
  • In-process inspection: What are the SPC procedures? How often are parts checked during production?
  • Final inspection: What equipment is used? CMM, optical comparator, surface profilometer?
  • Calibration records: Are measuring instruments calibrated on schedule with traceable standards?
  • Non-conformance handling: How are defective parts identified, segregated, and dispositioned?
  • Document control: Are drawings and procedures at the correct revision level on the shop floor?

For a deep dive on inspection methods, see our quality inspection methods guide.

3. Measurement and Inspection Equipment

A well-equipped inspection room signals serious quality commitment. Look for:

  • CMM (Coordinate Measuring Machine) — essential for complex parts with GD&T requirements
  • Optical measurement systems — profile projectors, vision systems for 2D measurements
  • Surface roughness tester — confirms Ra values meet specifications
  • Hardness tester — if heat-treated parts are in scope
  • Thread gauges, pin gauges, ring gauges — for routine dimensional verification
  • Temperature-controlled inspection room — for high-precision work (±0.01 mm or tighter)

4. Material Management and Traceability

  • Material storage: Is raw material stored properly, labeled, and protected from contamination?
  • Lot traceability: Can they trace a finished part back to the original material lot?
  • Material certifications: Do they retain and provide material test reports (MTR) for each lot?
  • FIFO system: Is first-in-first-out practiced for material management?

5. Production Capacity and Planning

  • Current utilization: What percentage of capacity is committed? Over 85% means your orders may face scheduling pressure
  • Shift pattern: Single shift, double shift, or 24/7? This tells you about flexibility and maximum capacity
  • Production planning system: Is scheduling done with software or whiteboards? Both can work, but systematic approaches scale better
  • Lead time track record: What is their on-time delivery rate? Ask for data, not just claims
  • Bottleneck operations: Identify any secondary processes (heat treatment, plating) that are outsourced and could affect lead time

6. Workforce and Management

  • Operator skill level: How long have key operators been with the company? High turnover is a quality risk
  • Training records: Is there a documented training program for operators and inspectors?
  • Engineering capability: Do they have process engineers who can do DFM analysis and process optimization?
  • Communication: Can the team communicate effectively in your language? Language barriers cause specification misunderstandings
  • Management stability: Is the leadership team experienced and committed to the business long-term?

7. Environmental and Safety Compliance

  • Shop floor cleanliness: A clean, organized factory generally produces better quality parts
  • Safety practices: Are operators wearing appropriate PPE? Are machine guards in place?
  • Waste management: How are coolant, chips, and waste materials handled? Proper disposal signals responsible management
  • Environmental certifications: ISO 14001 is a plus, especially for regulated industries

Red Flags to Watch For

Certain observations during an audit should raise immediate concerns:

  • Reluctance to show certain areas — a transparent supplier opens every door
  • Discrepancy between claimed and actual equipment — the machine list doesn't match what's on the floor
  • No measuring equipment at operator stations — operators should be checking parts during production
  • Dirty or disorganized inspection room — if they don't care about their inspection environment, they may not care about accuracy
  • No documented procedures — verbal-only quality systems don't survive employee turnover
  • Excessive work-in-process inventory — signals poor production planning and potential quality issues
  • Claims of zero defects — every factory has defects; honest factories track and reduce them

Remote Audit Options

If visiting in person isn't feasible, you can conduct an effective remote audit by:

  • Live video walkthrough: Request a guided video tour via WeChat, Zoom, or Teams
  • Document review: Request and review quality manuals, procedures, calibration records, and inspection reports
  • Sample evaluation: Order trial parts and evaluate quality, packaging, documentation, and delivery performance
  • Third-party audit: Hire a local audit firm to conduct an on-site audit on your behalf

A remote audit combined with a trial order gives you about 70–80% of the confidence of an on-site visit. For high-value or safety-critical parts, however, an in-person audit is strongly recommended.

After the Audit: Making Your Decision

After completing your audit, score each area and create a clear comparison if you're evaluating multiple suppliers. Consider:

  • Are there any critical non-conformances that require corrective action before you place an order?
  • Are there minor findings you can work with the supplier to improve over time?
  • Does the overall capability match your quality and capacity requirements?
  • Is the management team responsive and open to improvement?
